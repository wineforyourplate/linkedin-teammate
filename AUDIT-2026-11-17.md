# Audit: the LinkedIn teammate as an engagement teammate (17 Nov 2026)

**Why this audit:** this pod is about to be the real deployment for engagement. New users sign in to
the app, connect their own LinkedIn, approve drafts, and switch auto-post on or off. No assistant
runs alongside it, so everything has to live in the pod: agents, notes, schedules and the app.

**How it was done:**
- Walked every onboarding screen and the Home, Engage and People pages as a new user, in the
  preview on demo data.
- Read every skill, function, schedule and table.
- Compared the pod with two weeks of running engagement every day by hand.
- After the changes, imported the bundle into a fresh pod on lemma.work, went through it as a new
  person, and smoke-tested a draft and an approval.

## 2. The auto-post switch, end to end

- **Where:** the top of the Engage page. Off by default.
  - Off: "I only draft. Nothing goes out until you approve it."
  - On: what it does, the day's limit, and that messages and connection requests always wait.
- **Stored as:** the engage skill row's `approval`. `each` means off; `within_limits` means on.
  This is the approval mode the agent's rules already recognised.
- **The clock** (`engage_clock`, every 15 minutes, per person):
  - It claims approved comments, plus, with auto-post on, the teammate's own drafts that are older
    than a 10-minute hold, so the person can still edit or skip them.
  - At most 8 in any hour.
  - Claimed drafts are marked `posted_via: "auto"`.
  - Replies to accounts with 10K+ followers never auto-post.
- **The posting run** asks `function_allowance` before each comment and waits out its
  `wait_seconds`.
  - After posting, it reloads the post and checks the comment shows.
  - A missing comment, a checkpoint or a warning stops everything. Rows go back to where they
    were, and the person gets one message.
- **Shown:** an "Auto" count on the switch, how many went out on their own in the daily summary,
  and an `auto` share on the scorecard.

## 3. Memory without an assistant

**What the agent reads before every comment, reply or DM** (`engage` §0):
1. Their lessons: `facts` with `section: "engage"`.
2. Their voice rules.
3. Their story bank.
4. Where people should go next.

It learns from any new edits first. The pod-wide standing notes in `/memory/AGENTS.md` are read
into every run automatically.

**How edits and approvals become notes** (`engage` §7):
- **Edits.** Each comment keeps `first_draft`, which never changes, beside `comment_draft`, the
  text that goes out.
  - At the start of every finding run, and every morning, the teammate compares the two on
    approved and posted rows and writes one lesson per rule.
  - A lesson holds the rule, where it applies, why, before and after, and its source.
  - Lessons are updated in place, never stacked; at most 25 are kept.
- **Skips.** Three alike in a week become a lesson ("fewer posts from recruiters").
- **Their words.** Anything they say in the app about comments becomes a locked lesson at once.
- **Results.** The weekly scorecard can add a lesson, labelled as such.
- **Shown and editable:** Personalization → How I comment. They can change any lesson in chat or
  forget it. The daily summary lists what was learned yesterday.

Post drafts already worked this way (`first_draft` vs `body` becomes voice rules). Comments now do
too.

## 4. Every person gets their own clock

Outside users will clone the teammate into their own org rather than join this pod. Either way,
the person who finishes onboarding needs timed jobs that run as them.

- The bundle's timed schedules ship switched off, as templates.
- Once someone finishes onboarding, the app creates that person's own switched-on copy of each:
  - named `<template>-u<id prefix>`;
  - visible only to them;
  - on their timezone.
- Their runs then see their rows, their browser and their LinkedIn connection.
- The copy is re-synced on every load: it re-creates a missing schedule and fixes drift from the
  template.
- Event schedules (approve → publish, confirm → learn, skill on → first run) already run as the
  person whose row changed, so they stay shared.
- A clone that brings someone else's copies (another id's suffix) has them switched off, never
  deleted, so nobody runs two clocks.
- Dev builds never create schedules unless `?clock` is in the URL.

## 5. What the lab taught, now in the pod

Written as default rules in the `engage` skill (§1, "The playbook"). Each rule keeps where it
applies and why. The person's own lessons override any of them.

| Rule | Where in the pod |
| --- | --- |
| 30 comments a day by default, 40 at most, spread unevenly by when fresh posts appear | `function_allowance`, Engage limits, `engage` §1.6 |
| At least 2 minutes between comments, 4 once 4 went out in an hour, never more than 8 in an hour | `function_allowance` (`wait_seconds`), `engage_clock`, `linkedin-browser` §4 |
| A new teammate starts at 10 a day, then 20, then the limit | `function_allowance` |
| Lanes in order: replies to them, asks, warm people, their world, climbing posts, fresh topic posts | `engage` §1.1 |
| Asks first: every real ask answered before other lanes, up to a day old (two for "looking for someone to build") | `engage` §1.1, §4.1 |
| Fresh posts: under 3 hours, 6 with traction; threads under ~50 comments, never 100+ | `engage` §1.1 |
| Open with the one gap in their post, by name | `engage` §1.2 |
| Real stories over setups; setups told plainly, never as a customer or a number | `engage` §1.3 |
| Show what they do, one step up the ladder (name, tag, link, the one thing to try), only where it fits | `engage` §1.4 |
| The 7-point self-check: its own point, no echo, specific, no template, tight, out loud, fits their lessons | `engage` §1.5 |
| Every comment checked under the post after posting | `engage` §1.7, §5 |
| Stop on any checkpoint, warning, login page or a comment that didn't show | `engage` §1.8, `linkedin-browser` §8, `function_allowance` |
| Skip bait posts that address "AI agents" | `engage` §1.1 |
| Reply hierarchy: hot, question, pushback, agree, ack, ignore | `engage` §3.3 |
| A DM or connection note for people who show interest, always for approval, 5 a day, one per person ever | `engage` §3.4, §6 |
| Daily summary shape: status, people, learned, for you | `daily-summary` |

The proposed rules from the daily reviews that were never approved went in as defaults where they
generalise:
- open with the gap;
- real over setups;
- one story once a day;
- short ask searches (long boolean ones come back empty);
- traction for late-night picks.

Rules that only made sense for one founder's account (named people, a specific company page, X
recovery limits) were left out.

## 6. Added for engagement

- **Reply handling** (`engage` §3), every 2 hours in working hours.
  - It reads back their comments and finds every new reply, on their comments and on their posts.
  - It gives each reply a signal and acts on it: react, one reply per person per thread.
  - People who show interest go on the Interested list.
  - Target: every reply answered within 2 hours.
- **The Interested list** (People → Interested).
  - Who said they'd try it or asked a question, in their own words, with a link to the thread.
  - Each has one drafted DM (1st-degree) or connection note, to approve, edit or skip.
  - Approved ones go out in the next browser session.
- **The daily summary** (`daily-summary`, in the morning run).
  - Yesterday's comments, and whether all of them showed.
  - Who replied, who showed interest and what's been done, what it learned, and what waits for
    them.
  - Shown as a card on Home and sent as one message.

## 7. Hosted and smoke-tested (17 Nov)

The bundle was imported into a fresh pod on lemma.work with `scripts/bootstrap.sh --import`. The
install declared the LinkedIn connector, imported the tables, functions, agent, workflows and
schedules, uploaded `/memory/AGENTS.md` and the templates, published 16 skills, restored the grants
and deployed the app. `pods doctor` reports no errors. The app is members-only: a visitor who isn't
signed in gets Lemma's sign-in page.

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Per-person schedules, using the app's own code (`src/lib/schedules.ts`) against the hosted pod | 8 personal copies created, active, `PERSONAL`, on the person's timezone. A second run changed nothing. A timezone change updated all 8. A fake copy under another id, as a clone might bring, was switched off. The test copies were then removed, so they're created for real when onboarding finishes |
| The engage clock, run as that person | `skip: still onboarding`. Nothing runs on a clock before onboarding is done |
| **One draft:** asked the hosted teammate in chat for a comment on a sample ask post | One `engagements` row: `proposed`, lane `ask`, `first_draft` = `comment_draft`, 420 characters, no em dash. It answered the question first and made its own point. It claimed no customer or number (none in their facts) and left out the pitch (no destination set) |
| **One approval:** edited the comment and approved it, with the same write the app's Approve button makes | `approved`. The clock held it (onboarding not done, engage off), so nothing reached LinkedIn |
| **Memory:** `[engage] learn` | Two `engage` lessons from that edit, each with where, why, before and after, and the source "your edit, 17 Nov". It also set `learned_at` and asked for a real story, since the bank was empty. Two lessons from one edit was too eager, so the skill now allows one narrow lesson per edit until a second edit confirms it |
| Spacing and ramp in `function_allowance` | First comment allowed. The next one, right after: `wait_seconds: 157`. A day-2 teammate's comment cap: 10 |

The test rows (the draft, the lessons, the activity row) were deleted afterwards. The person's
profile and 7 skill rows remain, as the app would create them, with onboarding not started.

## 8. Open items

1. **How outside users clone it.** Users will clone the teammate into their own org, not join this
   pod. Two things to settle:
   - **The GitHub copy is behind.** The repository behind the README's Install button doesn't have
     these changes until they're committed and pushed. A clone from GitHub today gets the old pod.
   - **After any clone, run `scripts/bootstrap.sh`.** It publishes the skills, uploads
     `/memory/AGENTS.md` and restores the grants.
2. **Templates read at runtime.** Each person's schedules are copied from the switched-off templates
   the app lists. This was tested as the pod's owner, which is what a cloner is. Shared pods with
   plain `POD_USER` members are untested.
3. **An engagement-first onboarding** (finding #12).
4. **De-duplicate skill rows** (finding #11).
5. **Load:** each person adds about 8 schedules, and the engage clock wakes every 15 minutes, almost
   always to do nothing. At 100 people that is about 10,000 cheap function runs a day. Fine for
   now; one shared tick per person is the obvious next step if it costs too much.
